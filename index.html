13.8 BILLION YEARS AGONOW

01 — 13.8 BILLION YEARS AGO

A beginning
without an outside.

The Big Bang describes the universe’s early hot, dense state and its expansion. In its first instants, space itself expanded extraordinarily fast — a period called inflation.

ONE SECOND

Light and particles filled a universe hotter than stars.

Open a cosmic footnote +

“Before” the Big Bang is not a settled scientific question. This chapter shows the observable universe’s earliest known history, not an explosion into an empty room.

02 — 380,000 YEARS LATER

The first light
gets loose.

As the universe cooled, electrons joined atomic nuclei. Light could finally travel freely, leaving behind a faint all-sky afterglow we can still detect: the cosmic microwave background.

THE OLDEST LIGHT

It is a snapshot of the universe as an infant.

Open a cosmic footnote +

Tiny temperature variations in this ancient light became the seeds of later cosmic structure — places where gravity could gather matter.

THE AFTERGLOW

03 — HUNDREDS OF MILLIONS OF YEARS LATER

Darkness learns
to spark.

After a long dark age, gravity pulled gas into the first stars and galaxies. Their intense light began transforming the hydrogen fog between galaxies.

NEW ELEMENTS

Stars forged heavier elements that would one day become rocks, oceans, and us.

Open a cosmic footnote +

There is no photograph of the first star. This scene is an original visual interpretation of the process scientists reconstruct from light, models, and deep-space observations.

COSMIC DAWN

04 — BILLIONS OF YEARS OF ASSEMBLY

Gravity draws
the first maps.

Stars gathered into galaxies, galaxies met and merged, and the universe became a web of luminous islands. One of those islands would become our Milky Way.

NOT A STILL LIFE

Galaxies grow through star formation, encounters, mergers, and time.

Open a cosmic footnote +

Cosmic history is not evenly paced: the early universe changed radically in short intervals, then individual systems evolved over billions of years.

NASA: Galaxies & cosmic structure ↗
A GALAXY IN MOTION

05 — 4.6 BILLION YEARS AGO

A cloud becomes
a solar system.

A spinning cloud of gas and dust collapsed into a young Sun and a flattened disk. Small bodies collided, gathered, and became the planets, moons, asteroids, and comets we know.

99.8% OF THE MASS

The Sun holds nearly all of the solar system’s mass.

Open a cosmic footnote +

The leftover fragments are valuable time capsules. Asteroids and comets preserve traces of the solar system’s earliest construction.

NASA: How the solar system formed ↗
THE SOLAR NEBULA

06 — 4.5 BILLION YEARS AGO

A world of lava
finds its moon.

Earth formed from swirling material around the young Sun. It was hot, battered, and volcanic. A giant collision likely hurled debris into orbit; over time, that debris became the Moon.

NOT YET BLUE

Early Earth had no solid surface, oceans, or breathable air.

Open a cosmic footnote +

The details of lunar formation are still being tested, but leading models share a colossal impact as part of the Moon’s origin story.

NASA: Moon formation ↗
A NEW WORLD

07 — 4.0 TO 2.5 BILLION YEARS AGO

Small life changes
the sky.

Evidence suggests life was present on Earth by about 4 billion years ago. Much later, oxygen-producing microbes transformed the atmosphere in the Great Oxidation Event.

THE PLANET BREATHES

Oxygen altered chemistry, ecosystems, and the possible futures of life.

Open a cosmic footnote +

Early life did not need an oxygen-rich atmosphere. The rise of oxygen is a long, complex story, recorded imperfectly in rocks and chemistry.

NASA: How life first emerged ↗
OXYGEN RISES

08 — 541 MILLION TO 66 MILLION YEARS AGO

Life experiments
at planetary scale.

Complex life diversified in the oceans, forests remade the land, and dinosaurs dominated for more than 160 million years. Earth’s story is full of beginnings — and endings.

66 MILLION YEARS AGO

A mass extinction ended the age of non-avian dinosaurs and changed what came next.

Open a cosmic footnote +

Evolution is not a ladder. It branches, vanishes, adapts, and begins again as environments change.

NASA: Planet Earth through the ages ↗
CHANGE IS CONSTANT

09 — THE PRESENT MOMENT

A blue world
looks homeward.

In a narrow slice of cosmic time, humans learned to read the sky and leave Earth’s surface. Satellites now help us study the very planet that made observers possible.

ONE SHARED HOME

The Earth we see today is one frame in a 4.5-billion-year transformation.

Open a cosmic footnote +

The future is open. Every image of Earth from space is both a scientific record and a reminder that its atmosphere is thin, bright, and finite.
